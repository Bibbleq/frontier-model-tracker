Frontier Model Tracker — data

An independent, community-maintained history of frontier and significant AI model availability across the Microsoft AI stack. This page publishes the machine-readable outputs. The dataset in the repository is authoritative; everything here is generated from it and rebuilt on every change.

Files

  • events.jsonFull dataset: metadata, canonical events and the validation backlog
  • events.csvFlattened timeline, IDs resolved to display names
  • lag.csvDerived adoption lag. Read certainty before lag_days_min
  • validation-backlog.csvOpen research questions — not confirmed history
  • models.csvModel registry with aliases and families
  • surfaces.csvSurface registry with rename lineage and analytical tiers
  • status.jsonBuild totals, warnings and coverage gaps

Reading it correctly

Three distinctions do most of the work, and collapsing any of them produces a wrong answer.

FieldQuestion it answers
kindIs this an availability fact at all, or an announcement, policy change or milestone? Only availability belongs on a timeline.
lifecycleWhere in the release cycle: preview, GA, retired, suspended, restored.
exposureHow the model is exposed: underlying, specialist, catalogue, selectable or default.

Catalogue availability counts as Microsoft availability but never as Copilot availability. GitHub Models is not GitHub Copilot. A specialist experience is not the general model picker. An announcement of future availability is not first availability. These rules are enforced by the build, not left to the reader.

Lag is derived, never stored. A row may be exact, a range when a date is only known to a month, or one of three distinct unknowns — including unknown_open_research, which means the question is unresearched rather than the model absent.
